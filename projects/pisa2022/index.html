Research question and contribution

How is daily digital gaming associated with nine socio-emotional competencies, and what does an international perspective add? The Time Displacement Hypothesis provided a theoretical starting point: gaming may compete with activities supporting development, while also offering social experiences of its own. Duration alone cannot explain those experiences.

I developed the research question, prepared the questionnaire data in R, constructed the gaming measure, reverse-coded items, and calculated scale scores and reliability statistics. Under academic supervision, I implemented the imputation and multilevel workflow, prepared results and interpreted their implications. This portfolio version distinguishes original thesis estimates from later descriptive corrections.

Data and methods

The retained data contain 370,972 records from 48 countries and regions in each of ten imputed datasets. These are ten completed versions of one sample. Selection followed questionnaire coverage. Austria and Belgium lack observed emotional-control and trust scales, and the overall sample size is not a verified N for every regression.

Measurement. I combined weekday and weekend gaming responses into approximate daily hours, weighting them five and two times. Response categories received representative durations. Nine competencies were analysed separately, including empathy, cooperation, emotional control, self-control and perseverance.

Missing data. Daily playtime was missing for 20.48% of retained records. The thesis describes joint multilevel imputation with jomo/mitml, country clustering and auxiliary achievement and background variables. Multiple imputation represents uncertainty about missing values under its assumptions; it does not guarantee unbiased estimates.

Models. Country random intercepts allow baseline competency levels to differ. I compared unadjusted models with models including gender and HISEI, the highest parental occupational-status index. The thesis describes separate analyses across imputations and Rubin pooling.

The reported associations were small. Adjustment strengthened the negative coefficients for perseverance and emotional control, but weakened self-control and empathy. Stress resistance changed sign. Interpretation therefore depends on both the competency and the covariates included.

Descriptive context

Corrected country means range from 1.69 hours in Spain to 3.09 hours in Albania. The equal-country average is 2.32 hours; the average over student records within each imputation is 2.26 hours. Different country sample sizes explain the difference. Neither is a survey-weighted worldwide estimate.

The figure retains the original imputations: 2.69% of playtime entries across the completed datasets fall outside the coded 0–8-hour range. The correction fixes aggregation, not imputation. These values limit the plausibility of fine country comparisons.

Interpretation and limits

The original results broadly followed the expected negative direction, but do not establish gaming time as a strong explanation of socio-emotional differences. They are compatible with the Time Displacement Hypothesis without establishing the proposed mechanism. The duration measure does not capture game content, motives or social experiences.

Cross-sectional self-reports cannot establish causal direction. Country random intercepts accommodate different baselines but do not test country-specific slopes or cultural explanations. The surviving models omit the full PISA survey design and school-level clustering. Scoring and exclusion-mask issues need resolution before a new inferential analysis.

Further research should measure content, motives and social context, use repeated observations to investigate temporal ordering, and combine quantitative data with interviews or gaming diaries. These findings alone cannot justify playtime thresholds or recommendations for particular games.

Reproducibility and sources

I ran the original imputation on a university server. The production code was not copied before routine server cleanup; the methods description and imputed data were retained. The repository provides documented R examples and a reproducible descriptive figure, rather than a full reproduction of the original analysis. No new imputation or regression fitting was performed.
